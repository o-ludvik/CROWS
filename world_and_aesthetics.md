# What the crows say…? — World, Story & Aesthetics (Light Context)

> **Non-binding context.** This document describes the world, story, characters and visual vibe of the game. The demo can draw from it freely but does not have to follow it strictly. The gameplay & UI spec remains the source of truth for mechanics.
>
> Translated and condensed from the Czech GDD (sections *Shrnutí*, *Příběh a postavy*, *Průběh dnů* and *Estetika*).

## The Pitch

*What the crows say…?* (working title) is a narrative game that teaches teenagers critical thinking, fact-checking and spotting disinformation by working with news and messages.

The player is the **innkeeper** of an inn sitting right on the border of three neighboring kingdoms in a fictional European Middle Ages. It is a prestigious post. The innkeeper decides who may cross the border and who is turned away, and also which information gets passed on. Because they are the only person living at the tri-border, **they receive all the news from all three kingdoms.**

Themes running through the stories include **propaganda, disinformation and slander**, with the fictional world mirroring real-world situations. The tone aims for hard moral choices with no clearly right answer.

## The World

### Geography

- Three kingdoms are separated by mountain ranges and arranged in a **triangle**.
- The only passable route between them runs through **The Inn**, which lies in a depopulated valley roughly at the center of the triangle.

### History: The Greatest War

- Centuries ago, the border region was inhabited. Then came **The Greatest War**: one kingdom invaded and occupied another.
- Peace couldn't be restored for decades. It came only at enormous cost, when the third kingdom allied with resistance fighters from the aggressor's own land and with the opposition in the occupied kingdom, and together they defeated the aggressor.
- To prevent a repeat, the kingdoms signed a **pact** despite their disagreements. It requires the border region to stay depopulated so nobody can ever seize the crossing again.

### The Office of the Innkeeper

- The pact created the prestigious office of **innkeeper**. The innkeeper is the only person allowed to live permanently on the border, and they administer the crossing.
- **The election uses nine votes:**
  - 3 votes from representatives of the three kingdoms
  - 3 votes from the **Order of Innkeepers** (innkeepers from each kingdom)
  - 3 votes from the **avatars of the common people**, mythical figures nobody knows anything about, who appear only for the election
- The innkeeper must be an **impartial mediator** who judges whether a person may pass through or stay the night.
- Above all, the innkeeper is the only person with access to **all the kingdoms' news**.

### The Crows

Every morning, **crows fly in from each kingdom** carrying news about what happened the day before and what is expected to happen that day. Only the innkeeper knows everything, and it is up to them what to pass on to whom and what to keep to themselves.

### The Kingdoms (placeholder names)

The working document refers to the kingdoms as **P.**, **R.** and **Č./C.** These letters are loosely inspired by real-world states, used only as geopolitical and thematic inspiration. **The final game will give all three kingdoms their own invented names.** For the demo, invented names are preferred.

- **R.** is the expansionist kingdom. It bribes innkeepers, gathers troops under false pretexts and wages disinformation campaigns.
- **P.** is the most directly threatened neighbor. It tries to convince Č. that the threat is real.
- **Č.** is the reluctant third party. It wants nothing to do with the conflict, but in the end it is the one that keeps the pact alive.

## Story Overview

The core thread of the whole game is **kingdom R.'s attempt to start a war and conquer the other kingdoms, while the innkeeper tries to stop it.**

### Day Zero: The Demo Day

- **When it happens:** about 500 years after the pact, and one month before the main campaign. The Greatest War is now only a legend, and the innkeeper's office has lost most of its prestige.
- **Who the player is:** the **Old Innkeeper**, a seasoned host with an established network and a well-run business. He once truly wanted to be impartial, but over time he started taking bribes and meddling in the kingdoms' affairs. Today that catches up with him.
- **What is going on:** Kingdom R., which regularly bribes him, is massing soldiers on the border. Its crows report that its roads are damaged and being repaired, which is a cover story for the troop build-up.
- **How the day unfolds:**
  - A bribing scout from R. stops by and claims that workers will be repairing roads.
  - The player learns the truth (an army is gathering) from visual clues on characters, the information context and other guests' accounts.
  - The player then decides whether to report it to the other two kingdoms.
- **How it ends:** the innkeeper is **always arrested**. R. arrests him if he reports; P. arrests him if he doesn't, because P. finds out what he was doing. Either way, **the war is postponed.**
- **Goals of the day:**
  - Don't be manipulated by disinformation.
  - Protect your own life.
  - Stop disinformation spreading among the patrons.
- Day zero also serves as the **tutorial** and as the material for **school workshops**: it is played under expert guidance and followed by a group reflection.

### Interlude: One Month Later

The war is on hold and diplomatic negotiations are underway. The situation depends on the player's earlier choice:

- If he reported, R. accuses P. of arresting the innkeeper and stirring up unrest. P. counters that R. had been bribing him.
- If he didn't report, P. convinces Č. that this is the final proof R. is up to no good.

Č. eventually calms everyone down: the ancient pact still works, and together they will elect a new innkeeper.

### The Main Campaign (Days 1–7)

**The new innkeeper** is created by the player. In the story, they come from R.:

- As a young person, they supported the regime and fought in the war.
- They grew disgusted with the regime, and were pushed aside and silenced.
- They once saved the lives of spies from the other kingdoms. The spies offered them a job, which they refused.
- Years later, they were elected innkeeper. P. and Č. voted for them because of their independence; R. voted for them hoping to bribe them.

The campaign runs in three blocks:

| Days | What happens |
|---|---|
| **1–3** | **Introduction to the conflict, the backstory and the worldbuilding.** R. destroys the road on P.'s side, so no guests from P. arrive on days 1–2. R. prepares an invasion. The innkeeper foils it with help from individuals in Č. (the kingdom of Č. itself wants no part of it) and repairs the road to P. By day 3, guests from all three kingdoms arrive and P. knows what happened. |
| **4–5** | **R. launches an information war.** The big questions are what happened to the old innkeeper and who was bribing him. P. tries to convince Č. that the threat is real and war is coming. R. floods Č. with disinformation. These days introduce the core disinformation mechanics. |
| **6–7** | **The crow post stops.** Only one crow arrives on day 6, and none on day 7. The core mechanics flip: the innkeeper is in an information blackout and must rely on previously gathered information and the relationships they have built. One character may bring rescued crows on day 7 if the player completed their storyline. |

## Characters

These are early character concepts, and more may exist elsewhere.

**The Old Bachelor**
- A joker and a drinker who is kind to everyone.
- He gives the player tips and tricks.
- He finds the Local Gossip hilarious.

**The Local Gossip**
- She can't remember her own name.
- She knows every half-truth in the area. As a mechanic, her claims contradict information from other visitors and from the crows.
- She can't stand the Old Bachelor.

**The Messenger of Peace (Samael)**
- He is taciturn and disguised as an ordinary person.
- He carries a secret message.
- He dislikes the Old Bachelor.
- People fear him because of his hulking build.

**The Runaway Wife**
- She is fleeing a husband who beats her.
- She doesn't trust men.
- As a mechanic, she reacts differently if the innkeeper is a woman.

**The Messenger from R.**
- He regularly visits, bullies and bribes the old innkeeper.
- He tries the same with the new one.

**The Hospitaller**
- An angel? A knight?
- He has heard about the innkeeper from the old campaign.

**The Owner of Truth**
- He always arrives last and holds up a mirror to the player as a kind of reality check.
- He is aware of how much information the player is processing and confronts them about it.
- He is usually drunk, so the innkeeper can always brush him off.

## Aesthetics & Vibe

### The Inn (Exterior)

- A fairly large building for an inn, and a **former stable**.
- A **river runs around or through it**, giving a composition of tower, bridge and inn with the river flowing beneath.
- The chimney sits in the middle of the adjoining room (the lounge).
- There is a **circular window** above the main gates.
- The big gates are **not** the main entrance to the inn.
- There is currently no access to the bridge from the interior.

### The Innkeeper's POV (Interior)

- **The counter:** the innkeeper sits behind it. The counter covers a large part of the bottom of the screen, and the information management happens there. The player sees only their own hands resting on the counter.
- **The main room:**
  - It is long, with **three larger round tables** for guests.
  - Double gates stand on the far wall.
  - The lounge branches off it, though not at a right angle.
- **The tables:** well-worn, with chunks cut off here and there. One might even have a bite mark, or a tooth stuck in it.
- **The double gates (potentially important for the ending):**
  - They are visibly unused, barricaded with stuff, and maybe wrapped in small chains.
  - A big poster or board hangs over them. Guests use it to tally rounds, carve games and throw knives.
  - Barrels, an unused stool and coats of arms stand in front of them.
- **The circular window:**
  - It sits high above the gates, and every morning the kingdoms' crows fly in through it with letters.
  - It doubles as a **time indicator**: time advances after each guest, and visible **light rays** fall on different parts of the inn over the day.
  - Straw and twigs left by the crows lie at the bottom of the window.

### The Lounge

- It is the side room, with a much **cosier vibe** than the main room.
- It is darker and lit by the fireplace, with benches, musical instruments, spots for drying clothes and places to eat.
- **The fireplace** dominates the room. It is round and in the center, and it bathes the whole room in warm orange light. A sack or bucket of coal sits next to it.

### The Player Character

- Only the hands are ever visible.
- The hands could allow skin tone and gender to be changed, or be designed as gender-ambiguous.

### NPC Look: The Old Bachelor

- He is old but spry, an alcoholic, in loose and dirty clothes.
- The visual reference used for him was *The Old Man and the Sea*. That artwork was inspiration only and isn't the team's own.

### Map of the Kingdoms

- Three kingdoms sit in a **triangle**, with the inn roughly in the middle.
